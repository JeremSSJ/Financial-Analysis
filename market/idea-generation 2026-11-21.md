2. **Five of the eight fell for the same reason: fear that AI will disrupt them.** Those five are Booking, Adobe, Wolters Kluwer, Accenture and Experian. Buying several of them is really one bet. If you want more than one, pair them with names that fell for other reasons:

That is the kind of event-driven, non-fundamental drop you asked about.
4. **The market itself is supplying the event.** The S&P 500 is −11.6% from its 6 Oct record and about 16.6x forward earnings, the lowest in more than three years. The Fed is hiking. The French 10-year yield is near 4.9%. The watchlist in section 7 gives a "buy zone" price for 15 quality names that have *not* fallen yet.
5. **Rejected despite big falls:**

   - Alphabet and Meta: the business model is changing. Free cash flow is negative, buybacks have stopped, and both are issuing new shares.

## 1. Criteria and method

**Your criteria:**
- Long only.
- Style: quality at a fair price, plus value.
- Universe: global large and mid caps.
- Simple, easy-to-understand business models.
- "Fallen quality" where the fall doesn't look lasting, plus quality names not yet fallen that would be worth buying if an event dropped them without touching the fundamentals.

**Three research passes plus the vault:**
- European fallen quality: ~60 names, over €5bn.
- US fallen quality: ~60 names, over $5bn.
- A buy-on-dip watchlist: ~35 names.
- Leads from the vault (section 11).

**Filters:**

| Filter | Test |
|---|---|
| Fallen | ≥20% below the 52-week high, or clearly lagging the index in 2026. The S&P 500 is +0.9% YTD and the STOXX 600 about +5% |
| Quality | Return on capital above ~15% (US) or ~12–15% (Europe) through the cycle. Operating margin steady for 5 years. Net debt below ~2.5x EBITDA |
| Simple | You can say how it makes money in two sentences |
| Excluded | Banks, insurers, biotech, early-stage tech, commodity producers |
| Valuation | Forward P/E against the company's own history, plus FCF yield |
| Drop test | Name the cause and date of the fall, then argue both sides: temporary vs lasting |

**Implied growth (my addition)** [J]. A quick check of what the price already assumes is: *implied perpetual FCF growth ≈ 9% required return − FCF yield*. When that number is far below the company's actual growth, the market is pricing in a decline that hasn't happened yet.

**Data limits:**
- **"Own average" multiples aren't all the same measure:**
  - Europe: the average of year-end *trailing* P/Es for 2021–25, from MarketScreener. Comparing that with today's *forward* P/E slightly overstates the discount.
  - US: the mean of five year-end *forward* P/Es, from stockanalysis.
  - Each is labelled where it's used.
- **No peer averages:** sector-median data wasn't available, so the "vs" column compares each company with its own history.
- **US EV/EBITDA:** it is trailing (TTM), because no forward consensus was found.
- **Single checks:** the research ran out of its web-search allowance partway through. A few fields are n/a, and some figures were checked against only one source.

- **Catalysts:** FY26 results on 3 Feb 2027; CagriSema US launch in early 2027 if approved.

**Key risks:** this drop is **partly lasting** [J], which is why it ranks last.
- US list prices fall from 1 Jan 2027: about −50% for Wegovy and −35% for Ozempic.
- DKK 8.7bn of pipeline write-downs in Q3.
- The CFO leaves at year-end.
- Lilly is winning share.
- Fundsmith sold out in Q2 (vault).

**Next steps:** model 2027 volume growth against the price cuts. It needs more fundamental work than any other name on the list.
